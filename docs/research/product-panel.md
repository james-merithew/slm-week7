# Product Panel — Product / Behavioral Econ / Market Game Theory

> 2026-08-17. Robustness check: does the product view overturn the A-v2.1 pick?
> **Verdict: A stands — reinforced, not overturned.** Why-now addendum pending.

## Product lens

- **A (Vocab Lock v2.1):** buyer is parents of ESL/ELL + middle-school kids and
  adult career-switchers (already paying $15–50/mo); underrated second market:
  regulated plain-language contexts (patient education, compliance training)
  where "provably never used an undefined term" is a procurement feature.
  **Wedge:** prompted models drift and can't tell you what *you* have and
  haven't seen — A's persistent per-learner lexical state can show a receipt.
  "The *constraint* is not a product; the *ledger* is." With the ledger
  surfaced, the only candidate with a product-shaped skeleton (state,
  progression, receipts).
- **B (No-Praise):** real demand but zero defensibility — it's a tone toggle any
  competitor ships in an afternoon. "The market verdict and the gate verdict are
  the same verdict."
- **C (Gate):** viable only as buyer≠user (teachers mandate the friction);
  standalone = churn machine. A mode, not a product.

## Behavioral economics

- **Ledger repair is sound** — endowed progress (Nunes/Drèze) with the rare
  property that the progress is *real* (ledger = true learning state). Needs a
  visible goal: distance-to-tier ("14/20 → Circuits II"), or goal-gradient never
  activates.
- **Dissolves the patronizing critique:** progression framing makes simplicity
  read as *pacing* (Duolingo effect). "≤2 terms/turn" becomes rationing —
  scarcity that makes unlocks feel earned.
- **Dark-pattern guardrails:** no decay/loss-framing; don't farm the metric;
  "mastered" tier only when the *student* later uses the term correctly
  (retrieval-validated).
- **Other native fits:** variable reward (which term unlocks next depends on the
  conversation), session commitment, peak-end session recap, IKEA effect. Skip
  streaks; per-topic tiers are the right container.
- **C's rescue** (for the record): Ulysses contract — student pre-commits to
  explain-first mode + a scarce skip budget. Proves C is a toggle, not a product.

## Market game theory

- **The checker is plausibly worth more than the model.** Weights: ~zero moat.
  The deterministic checker + published gloss format is a *scoreable standard* —
  Flesch-Kincaid analogy: the formula outlived every product built on it.
- Adopters: ed-tech vendors certifying to districts, patient-education tools
  (health-literacy mandates), plain-language government comms (Plain Writing
  Act), corporate L&D. Standards cascade via procurement.
- Real switching cost: per-student lexical state (the ledger data).
- Defense-room effect: "deterministic checker beats LLM-judge; the eval outlives
  the model" is the engineering-credible spiky position.

## Three cheap mechanics to bake in this week (no checker/pipeline changes)

1. **Render the ledger** — surface the unlock set (already tracked) as an
   "earned vocabulary" panel in demo/transcripts, with per-topic count toward a
   named tier.
2. **Win-frame the unlock moment in training-data phrasing** — "that's yours
   now — I'll use it from here on." (Gloss still matches published form.)
3. **Session-end ledger recap turn** — peak-end rule; endowment display, demo
   moment, and defense evidence at once.

## Why now (2026)

1. **Small-model economics matured** — sub-8B fine-tunes run on-device/on-prem
   cheaply; schools and low-connectivity districts can deploy without per-token
   cloud spend or student-data egress, and a narrow behavioral contract is
   exactly what small models hold reliably.
2. **The auditability turn in procurement** — post-2024 AI-in-schools backlash
   means districts buy *verifiable guarantees*; a deterministic compliance
   checker is a procurement document, not a demo.
3. **Frontier commoditization** — capability is a commodity; the remaining
   wedges are enforceable behavioral contracts + persistent per-learner state —
   precisely A's two assets (checker + ledger).
4. **Plain-language/accessibility mandates** expanding into ed-tech and patient
   comms — a compliance buyer that didn't exist five years ago.

**Why NOT now (state honestly if asked):** frontier models with cheap long-lived
memory may soon track a learner's vocabulary frontier well enough that
"provable" beats "pretty good" only in regulated niches.
