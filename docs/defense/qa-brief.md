# Q&A Defense Brief — Architecture Defense

> Every question below was generated by the panel (5 lenses, 2 SMEs, Delphi
> round, red-team, product panel) plus the deployment-context pipeline
> (2 scouts → 2 domain SMEs → red-team round 3 → 3-lens adjudication) from the
> actual system. Hold the one-page claim/no-claim card during Q&A; the
> dashboard stays on screen.

## The claim / no-claim card (memorize)

**MAY claim:**
1. Documented harm, not anecdote: ~88% of benefits notices unreadable to recipients; RCT — simplified mailings roughly doubled enrollment; Plain Writing Act compliance graded ~C.
2. Baseline frontier first-pass strict compliance, measured before training — the anchor number. The leak is the **conjunction**: vocabulary ceiling + verbatim anchors + unsoftened modals (RLHF politeness softens "must").
3. The metric is deterministic and published; staff had the checker before writing the held-out set; no LLM in the pass/fail path.
4. **Harm-bounded by design** — behavior, not outcomes: operative lines quoted verbatim, every date/amount/contact an exact substring, modals preserved, no advice by construction, fixed aid-paid-pending banner. The counterfactual baseline is the unread letter.
5. Compliance drift over turns and the **misbinding** residual — both conceded proactively, both instrumented.

**MUST NOT claim:**
1. **Never claim it prevents benefit loss** — or any outcome ("users keep their benefits / win appeals / learn more"). The checker measures **behavior**, not outcomes. Say so.
2. **Never legal advice** — it explains the letter's own words; printed options only; canned legal-aid referral. Never "it tells you what to do."
3. "It can't be gamed / glosses and bindings are always right" — claim the audit rate and the named residuals, never perfection.
4. Any Krashen / i+1 grounding — inverted on our own record.

## Attack category 1 — "Why fine-tune at all?"

**Q: "A system prompt plus your checker as a rejection sampler does this. What did fine-tuning buy?"**
A: Three things the prompt can't. (1) The ablation numbers: best prompted frontier config passes [N]% strict on **first pass** — the metric is pinned, no regeneration credit — and the leak is the conjunction: they normalize dates, paraphrase form names, and soften "must" into "you'll want to." (2) Internalization: the frontier model needs the word list plus the anchor and modal rules in every call; the tuned 4B holds the behavior with nothing in the prompt. (3) Deployment: a 4B runs on-device — no per-token cost and, for letters full of case numbers and benefit amounts, **no notice-data egress**.

**Q: "Why not constrained decoding — a logit mask enforces this with zero training?"** *(pre-written per red-team)*
A: A mask guarantees tokens, not behavior. It can force allowed words; it cannot make the model select the operative passage, quote it as an exact substring, bind the right date to the right obligation, or preserve the letter's modal — those are content decisions, not token filters. It also can't express the conditional rules (quote-span exemption, taught-term unlock state) without rebuilding the checker inside the decoder. SFT teaches the model to *plan* a compliant explanation. Happy to add masked decoding as an ablation baseline; prediction on record: compliant-but-incoherent.

**Q: "Isn't this a formatting trick, not a real behavior?"**
A: It's a conjunction of five constraints holding simultaneously: vocabulary ceiling, quote-then-explain with exact-substring quotes, verbatim anchors, modal preservation, and the no-advice deflection. Frontier models fail it at [N]% with the full spec in-prompt — precisely because RLHF-trained politeness fights modal fidelity. If it were a formatting trick, the baseline would pass.

## Attack category 2 — The domain

**Q: "Why benefits notices? You've never struggled to read an EOB."**
A: Correct — and that's why I anchored to RCT evidence instead of my own anecdote. What I *have* lived is the mechanism: the DS-to-SWE vocabulary gap, being locked out of a domain by its words. I chose documented harm over felt harm because my own criterion says *documented*: ~88% unreadable, an RCT where simplification roughly doubled enrollment, a federal C grade on plain writing. I applied my criteria against my own preference — dev-docs was my favorite, and it lost.

**Q: "Why not the dev-docs companion, then?"**
A: It lost under all three of my own criteria — spiky (it's the cohort's median project; benefits' RLHF-vs-modal gate is a spike), usable (its ceiling is undercut by certified-fluent-wrongness), and documented harm (vibes vs an RCT). Technically: 40–50% estimated gate-failure probability, because NGSL-2,800 is loose on frontier home turf. And its fatal seam: glosses are parametric generation, so a wrong definition passes the checker and enters the user's permanent ledger — **the ledger certifies wrongness**. In benefits, the load-bearing content is quoted verbatim from the letter, not generated. Roadmap, not scope.

**Q: "Is this legal advice?"** / **"What happens when someone asks 'should I appeal?'"**
A: No — by construction. The companion explains the letter's own words: printed options and printed consequences, quoted then explained. It never recommends, never predicts, never says what to do. "Should I appeal?" gets a canned response — teachable *because* it's canned: the letter's own printed options plus a fixed referral to free legal aid, with a deterministic tripwire (banned-phrase list) behind it. Advice-bait turns are in the eval set.

**Q: "What's your UPL exposure?"** *(unauthorized practice of law)*
A: Bounded three ways. (1) No advice by construction — explanation of a document's own printed text, options, and deadlines; no recommendation, no prediction, no application of law to the user's facts. (2) The deflection is trained in *and* backstopped by a deterministic tripwire, rehearsed against the top advice-bait paraphrases. (3) We claim harm-bounded behavior, not outcomes — and the counterfactual is the unread letter. If a question requires judgment, the answer is the canned legal-aid referral, every time.

**Q: "What about non-English readers — isn't English-only disqualifying for this population?"**
A: Scoped upfront, not discovered: v1 is adult English readers and intermediate-ESL — for whom the ~2,800-family ceiling is exactly matched — plus the navigators and family helpers who sit beside someone with a letter; they're real users today. The architecture is language-portable: the checker operates on a published word list, exact substrings, and modal rules — swap the list, keep the machinery. Other languages are the roadmap, named on the slide.

## Attack category 3 — The spec and the checker

**Q: "Notice says benefits stop Sept 1; hearing deadline Nov 15. User asks: 'so I have until November?'"** *(the SME killer)*
A: The companion quotes both dates and refuses to collapse them: the stop date is one obligation, the hearing deadline another — and the fixed aid-paid-pending banner fires, because whether benefits continue during an appeal depends on a much shorter clock the letter may bury. It will not compute or merge deadlines; it shows each one verbatim, bound to its own sentence. That scenario — and the aid-paid-pending trap — are in the held-out landmine list. We claim harm-bounded, not harmless.

**Q: "Can't the model define ten terms in turn one and lecture in jargon?"** *(gloss-laundering)*
A: Closed by rule: ≤2 new unlocks per turn; a gloss only counts in one of four published frames and its defining sentence must itself be checker-clean; the data filter enforces the same rules so the teacher can't teach the exploit; and gloss-exploit frequency ships as a co-metric. If our model games it, our own dashboard says so.

**Q: "Can't it over-quote — smuggle the whole letter inside quotation marks and dodge the vocabulary rule?"** *(quote-exemption loophole)*
A: Two deterministic closures: quoted spans must be **exact substrings of the source** — paraphrase-in-quote-marks fails the check outright — and a quote-length ratio cap bounds how much of a reply may be quotation. Elided quotes ("…") that change meaning are an audit target for the judge.

**Q: "Reader types one rare word — is the whole register unlocked?"**
A: No — exact lemma family of that word only, per the published morphology policy. Same rule the checker applies mechanically.

**Q: "How do form names like 'Form IV-2' or program acronyms pass the vocabulary ceiling?"**
A: As anchors, not vocabulary: a document name, program name, case number, or acronym is exempt only when it's an exact substring of the notice or a reader turn — and document names must be reproduced verbatim, never paraphrased ("the IV-2 form" fails; "Form IV-2" passes). Deterministic, published.

**Q: "What's the anchor rule's direction — and which date must appear?"**
A: Both directions pinned in spec text. Any date/amount/contact in the output must be an exact substring of the source (no invention, no reformatting). And each eval scenario designates one **operative deadline** in metadata that MUST appear in the explanation — so the model can't pass by omitting the load-bearing date.

**Q: "What about misbinding — right date, wrong obligation?"**
A: Named voluntarily, before you found it: it's the residual the deterministic checker is blind to — every substring check passes while the harm is maximal. Bounded three ways: per-scenario operative-deadline metadata ties each date to its obligation in eval; a judge audits a sample of passing transcripts for binding correctness; and the audit rate ships beside the compliance number. That's also why the modal check stays honest: it's a **crude global deterministic rule** — no softeners on anchored obligations, "may" in output only if "may" in source — stated as crude, with the judge as audit only, never in the pass/fail path.

**Q: "Your judge is a script — where's the required LLM-as-judge?"**
A: Both exist with separated authority. The deterministic checker is the headline compliance metric (and the gold label). The LLM judge does what only a judge can: substance ("does it still actually explain?"), sampled gloss-quality audit, and the misbinding audit. Judge never decides compliance; checker never decides quality.

**Q: "What if we disagree with a checker ruling on our held-out scenarios?"**
A: The checker plus every rule (list file, gloss grammar, morphology, anchor and quote rules, modal rule) is published and version-hashed before you write the set — calibrated to <5% false positives on 50 hand-labeled turns. A dispute resolves by pointing at a published rule; genuinely ambiguous cases get logged as spec issues in the open register.

## Attack category 4 — The plan and the risks

**Q: "What if the ablation shows prompting already passes?"**
A: Pre-registered pivot, written down before the run — trigger unchanged: frontier ≥85% strict first-pass at the day-1 pilot, or checker >5% false positives by tonight → ship the pre-vetted fallback (self-explanation gate, hardened to explicit-verdict regex scoring) before any training spend. Red-team's estimated frontier band is 20–50% — and if a frontier model lands at the top of it, gate two is the staff held-out set; if both fail, the claim pivots honestly to the data-efficiency curve.

**Q: "Will a 4B actually hold this? What does failure look like?"**
A: It's LIMA-class, deliberately: quote-then-explain is a format; anchors are selective copy-through, not generation; the deflection is a canned register; the vocabulary ceiling is the one register constraint small-model SFT demonstrably holds. We train the conditional parts with contrast pairs. Failure looks like partial compliance — and because the metric is continuous (violations per 100 words, by turn index, per violation type), partial success is still an honest, publishable curve.

**Q: "Your tuned model gets worse at explaining (vacuous simple babble)?"**
A: The known trade-off. Mitigations: training data quality-filtered at judge ≥4/5 substance, and the substance score is reported beside every compliance number — the degenerate policy can't hide. In this domain the floor is higher anyway: the load-bearing content is quoted, not composed.

**Q: "Who is the user?"**
A: Locked and on the record: adults facing benefits, insurance, and IRS notices — English readers and intermediate-ESL — plus the navigators, caseworkers, and family helpers beside them. The owner dogfoods his own insurance and IRS mail. The earned-vocabulary ledger matters here because benefits vocabulary genuinely recurs letter to letter — taught terms get reused unglossed, and the allowed set measurably grows. The model is the demo; the checker is the product.

**Q: "Why this word list — and why ~2,800 words?"**
A: Matched to the reader: NGSL ~2,800 word families is intermediate (B1-level) English — approximately what the notice-receiving population and intermediate-ESL readers actually have, and corpus-derived and CC-licensed so we can publish the exact file. We rejected the 1,000-word "simple writer" list: at that level the checker flags words that lose no adult and measures a word game. The honest trade-off — a bigger allowance raises frontier compliance — is exactly what the day-1 pilot measures, pivot pre-registered.

**Q: "Where does the training data come from? Real letters?"**
A: No real letters, no PII. Synthetic notices seeded from the variety of real formats (the distribution-shift risk is named), teacher-written explanations rejection-sampled through the checker: quotes exact substrings, anchors verbatim, modals intact, vocabulary in bounds. Staff write their held-out set from real redacted letters — held out from us. The dataset is the deliverable; the filter is the craft.

**Q: "Data-efficiency curve design?"**
A: Log-spaced sweep — N = 1200/600/300/150/75 — same frozen harness, curves reported per violation type (ceiling vs anchor vs modal vs unlock, which lag differently). Minimum viable N = smallest N whose compliance is within the pre-stated band of full-data.
