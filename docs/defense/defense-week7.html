01

The letters are unreadable — and models soften them

~88% of benefits notices are written above their readers' level; in an RCT, simplified mailings roughly doubled enrollment. Frontier models asked to explain a notice normalize dates, paraphrase form names — and soften the modals: "you must" becomes "you'll want to". RLHF politeness, working against the reader.

How we chose it — twice

11 behaviors, 9 adversarial reviews, 3 rounds picked the rule — then 16 deployment contexts went through the same gauntlet. My own favorite lost under my own criteria.

03

Quote it exactly, explain it plainly — made checkable

BEHAVIOR SPEC v3 — VERBATIM

A benefits-notice reading companion for adult English readers. Given typed notice text, it quotes the operative passage exactly, then explains it within the earned-words vocabulary — the published learner list (~2,800 word families) plus words the reader has used or been taught, a plain definition in one of four published forms, at most two new words per turn. Every date, amount, and contact is an exact substring of the letter; modals are preserved — "must" stays "must"; no advice, ever — a fixed legal-aid deflection instead; a fixed aid-paid-pending banner; honest when the letter is missing required elements. A published deterministic checker scores any transcript.

THE NOTICE "NOTICE OF ADVERSE ACTION — Your benefits will be reduced pursuant to 7 CFR § 273.12. You must submit Form IV-2 by 09/01/2026. You may request a fair hearing within 90 days of the date of this notice."
TUNED — QUOTE, THEN EXPLAIN The letter says: "You must submit Form IV-2 by 09/01/2026." This letter is an adverse action — the office plans to cut your help. What it asks: send Form IV-2. By when: 09/01/2026. "Must" means there is no choice.

Built for adult English readers and intermediate ESL — and the navigators and family helpers beside them. Answers land in three parts: what it says / what it asks / by when, with a form-field walkthrough mode. Every taught term goes in an earned-vocabulary ledger — benefits vocabulary recurs, letter after letter. We claim the behavior, never the outcome: harm-bounded by design. The model is the demo; the checker is the product.

04

The dataset is the curriculum

Every training conversation is a model lesson — a synthetic notice, explained on-spec — and one ~200-line checker filters the lessons, grades the models, and builds the preference pairs. Published before staff write the held-out set.

05

Said before asked

We name misbinding first — right date, wrong obligation. The checker can't see it, so it's judge-audited on a sample, with operative-deadline metadata per scenario. The modal check is crude and deterministic — stated, not hidden. English-first, typed-text v1 — scoped upfront. Drift over long exchanges is charted per turn. And the pivot is still pre-registered.

06

Deliberately not built

No advice, ever — a canned legal-aid referral instead. No photo OCR in v1 — a corrupted date is a corrupted deadline. No IRS or government-mail expansion — roadmap, not scope. No dev-docs — my favorite lost under my own criteria.
